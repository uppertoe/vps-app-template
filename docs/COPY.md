# Writing for the app

Write the way one educated Australian speaks to another. The people reading
are usually senior clinicians, so assume intelligence and do not explain the
obvious.

- Use Australian English spelling and idiom.
- Write full, natural sentences. Short is good; clipped is not. Avoid
  fragments, headings phrased as questions, exclamation marks and chatty
  phrasing.
- Give every sentence its own subject, named in full. "Pending enquiries have
  been opened but not yet answered", not "Opened but not yet answered".
- Choose verbs that name what actually happens: saved, sent, shown, listed,
  refused. Avoid figures of speech that could mean something else.
- Say what to do and where the button is. Do not justify the design or
  narrate how the page works.
- When something has not happened, say so plainly from the reader's side:
  "The note has not been saved." Avoid words such as "failed" that suggest
  the reader broke something.
- Introduce a remedy with the condition under which it applies: "If you need
  access, you can contact the department."
- Button text names the action: "Save the note", "Send", "Sign out".

## Examples

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Oops! Something went wrong. | Something went wrong on our side. Please try again in a moment. |
| Access denied. | You do not have access to this page. |
| No notes yet! | No notes have been saved yet. |
| Submit | Save the note |
